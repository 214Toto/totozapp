1. Give at least 5 contra-indications for antipsychotic drugs (5 marks)

Model Answer

  • Known hypersensitivity to the drug
  • Comatose states
  • Severe depression (relative contraindication)
  • Parkinson's disease (due to dopamine blockade)
  • Severe hypotension or hypertension
  • Liver impairment (many antipsychotics metabolised in liver)
  • Bone marrow depression (especially with clozapine)

2. Antipsychotic drugs interact with other drugs, describe briefly on 2 of these interactions. (2 marks)

Model Answer

  • Alcohol: Additive CNS depression, increased sedation and risk of respiratory depression.
  • Anticholinergics: May reduce extrapyramidal side effects but can worsen anticholinergic effects (dry mouth, constipation, confusion).
  • Lithium: Drugs that reduce renal clearance (e.g., NSAIDs) increase lithium levels and toxicity risk.
  • Drugs that prolong QT: Antipsychotics (especially haloperidol, thioridazine) can prolong QT interval; co-administration with other QT-prolonging drugs increases risk of arrhythmias.

3. A 23-year-old man with Tourette's syndrome (15 marks)

Model Answer

(i) How is psychosis caused?
Psychosis is thought to be caused by a combination of genetic vulnerability, neurotransmitter imbalances (especially dopamine hyperactivity in the mesolimbic pathway), and environmental triggers (stress, substance abuse).

(ii) 3 positive & 2 negative symptoms:
Positive: Hallucinations (auditory, visual), delusions (false beliefs), disorganised speech/behaviour.
Negative: Social withdrawal, flat affect (emotional blunting).

(iii) Best drug: Haloperidol (or risperidone). Haloperidol is a typical antipsychotic often used for Tourette's.

(iv) Mechanism of action: Antipsychotics block dopamine D2 receptors in the brain (mesolimbic pathway for antipsychotic effect, nigrostriatal for EPS).

(v) 3 side effects of haloperidol: Extrapyramidal symptoms (tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia), sedation, hyperprolactinemia (gynecomastia, amenorrhea).

(vi) Involuntary movements (tardive dyskinesia): These are involuntary choreoathetoid movements of the face, tongue, and extremities. They arise from chronic D2 receptor blockade leading to up-regulation of dopamine receptors and super-sensitivity.

(vii) Treatment: Reduce dose or switch to an atypical antipsychotic. Use of tetrabenazine or deutetrabenazine may be considered. VMAT2 inhibitors are specific treatments for tardive dyskinesia.
