B1. Comment on the use of antimicrobial agents in line with the combination of bactericidal and bacteriostatic antimicrobial agents [2 Marks]

Model Answer

Combining bactericidal and bacteriostatic agents is generally avoided because bacteriostatic agents (e.g., tetracyclines, macrolides) can interfere with the action of bactericidal drugs that require active bacterial growth (e.g., penicillin, aminoglycosides). This can lead to antagonism. However, in some cases, such as in the treatment of certain enterococcal infections, combinations may be used synergistically, but caution is advised.

B2. Patient GM was recently admitted ... itchy rash suspected to be precipitated by one of the drugs. Which group of antimicrobials would you suspect and justify your answer? [2 Marks]

Model Answer

Suspected group: Penicillins (or cephalosporins).
Justification: Penicillins are the most common cause of drug-induced rashes (macular or urticarial). They can cause hypersensitivity reactions ranging from mild rashes to severe anaphylaxis. Given the timing after exposure, it is a likely culprit.

B3. Give an highlight on vancomycin as in: a. The class it belongs to [0.5 marks] b. General mode of action [0.5 marks]

Model Answer

a. Class: Glycopeptide antibiotic.
b. Mode of action: Inhibits cell wall synthesis by binding to the D-alanyl-D-alanine terminus of the peptidoglycan precursor, blocking transpeptidation.

B4. List at least four side effects of tetracyclines [2 Marks]

Model Answer

  • GI disturbances (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea)
  • Photosensitivity
  • Tooth discoloration (in children)
  • Hepatotoxicity (with high doses, especially IV)
  • Renal impairment (increased BUN, especially with outdated tetracyclines)
  • Vestibular toxicity (dizziness, vertigo – with minocycline)

B5. Explain the mechanism by which isoniazid causes peripheral neuropathy [2 Marks]

Model Answer

Isoniazid induces peripheral neuropathy by interfering with pyridoxine (vitamin B6) metabolism. It competes with pyridoxal phosphate for binding to apoenzymes, leading to deficiency of active B6. This results in impaired synthesis of neurotransmitters and myelin formation, causing axonal degeneration and sensory-motor neuropathy.

B6. Explain the mechanism of action for fluconazole [2 Marks]

Model Answer

Fluconazole is an azole antifungal that inhibits the enzyme lanosterol 14α-demethylase (a cytochrome P450 enzyme), which converts lanosterol to ergosterol, an essential component of the fungal cell membrane. This leads to accumulation of toxic sterols and disruption of membrane integrity, inhibiting fungal growth.

B7. Patient CK ... acyclovir lesions disappear but return. Is it resistance, drug failure, or disease state? Explain [2 Marks]

Model Answer

This is likely a disease state (i.e., recurrent herpes infection) rather than drug resistance or failure. Acyclovir suppresses the active infection but does not eradicate the latent virus in neurons. The lesions return due to reactivation of the latent virus. Resistance would be suggested by lack of response during treatment, not by recurrence after stopping.

B8. In terms of anti-retroviral therapy, what is a tropism assay and what is its significance? [2 Marks]

Model Answer

A tropism assay determines whether a patient's HIV strain uses the CCR5 or CXCR4 co-receptor (or both) for entry into host cells. Its significance lies in guiding the use of CCR5 antagonists (e.g., maraviroc) – which are only effective against CCR5-tropic virus. It prevents wasting therapy and potential virologic failure.

B9. List at least four drugs that are known to interact with anti-retroviral drugs [2 Marks]

Model Answer

  • Rifampicin (reduces levels of protease inhibitors and NNRTIs)
  • St. John's Wort (induces CYP450, reduces antiretroviral levels)
  • Ketoconazole (increases levels of many antiretrovirals via CYP inhibition)
  • Omeprazole (affects absorption of some drugs like atazanavir)
  • Warfarin (antiretrovirals can alter INR)
  • Oral contraceptives (antiretrovirals may reduce efficacy)

B10. Briefly elaborate the use of ethambutol in children [2 Marks]

Model Answer

Ethambutol is a first-line anti-TB drug used in children. However, dose adjustment is critical because of the risk of optic neuritis (reversible if caught early). The standard dose is 15–20 mg/kg/day, and visual acuity testing is recommended when possible. It is generally well-tolerated; the main concern is monitoring for visual changes.

C1. Elaborate on the mechanism by which antimicrobial resistance develops and give examples of specific drugs aligned to your discussion [5 Marks]

Model Answer

Mechanisms of antimicrobial resistance:

  • Enzymatic inactivation: Production of β-lactamases (e.g., penicillinase) that hydrolyze penicillin – seen with S. aureus and E. coli. Example: Benzyl penicillin.
  • Target modification: Alteration of penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) – e.g., MRSA.
  • Decreased permeability: Change in porin channels reduces drug entry – e.g., carbapenem resistance in Pseudomonas.
  • Efflux pumps: Active extrusion of drug – e.g., tetracycline resistance.
  • Target overproduction: e.g., trimethoprim resistance via overproduction of dihydrofolate reductase.
  • Metabolic bypass: e.g., vancomycin resistance in enterococci (altered D-alanine-D-lactate).

C2. Discuss penicillinase inhibitors under the following: a. Examples [1 Mark] b. Mechanism of action [2 Marks] c. Indications [2 Marks]

Model Answer

a. Examples: Clavulanic acid, Sulbactam, Tazobactam.

b. Mechanism: They are structural analogues of β-lactam antibiotics. They irreversibly bind to and inactivate the β-lactamase enzymes (suicide inhibitors), thereby protecting the co-administered penicillin from hydrolysis.

c. Indications: Used in combination with penicillins to treat infections caused by β-lactamase producing organisms, e.g., Augmentin (amoxicillin + clavulanate) for respiratory and skin infections, piperacillin-tazobactam for hospital-acquired infections.

C3. What is the mechanism of action of Sulphamethoxazole and cite some of its side effects [5 Marks]

Model Answer

Mechanism: Sulphamethoxazole is a sulfonamide that acts as a competitive antagonist of para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA). It inhibits the bacterial enzyme dihydropteroate synthase, preventing the synthesis of dihydrofolic acid, a precursor for tetrahydrofolic acid. This disrupts nucleotide synthesis and bacterial growth.

Side effects:

  • Allergic reactions (rash, Stevens-Johnson syndrome)
  • Gastrointestinal disturbances (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea)
  • Crystalluria (prevented by adequate hydration)
  • Hepatotoxicity (rare but serious)
  • Haematological: agranulocytosis, aplastic anaemia, haemolytic anaemia (in G6PD deficiency)
  • Photosensitivity

C4. Discuss Nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors under the following: a. Examples [at least 4] [2 Marks] b. Mechanism of action [2 Marks] c. Indications [2 Marks] d. Side effects - at least four (align to specific drugs) [2 Marks] e. Interactions [2 Marks]

Model Answer

a. Examples: Zidovudine (AZT), Lamivudine (3TC), Tenofovir (TDF), Abacavir (ABC), Didanosine (ddI), Emtricitabine (FTC).

b. Mechanism: They are phosphorylated to active triphosphate forms that compete with endogenous nucleotides for incorporation into the growing viral DNA chain. They lack a 3'-OH group, causing chain termination and inhibition of reverse transcriptase.

c. Indications: Used as part of combination antiretroviral therapy (cART) for HIV-1 infection, and also for post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) and prevention of mother-to-child transmission.

d. Side effects (aligned):

  • Zidovudine – Bone marrow suppression (anaemia, neutropenia), myopathy
  • Didanosine – Pancreatitis, peripheral neuropathy
  • Abacavir – Hypersensitivity reaction (HLA-B*5701 associated)
  • Tenofovir – Nephrotoxicity, decreased bone mineral density
  • Lamivudine/Emtricitabine – Well-tolerated, mild GI upset

e. Interactions: NRTIs are generally not metabolised by CYP450, but interactions include:

  • Tenofovir + didanosine – increases didanosine levels (toxicity)
  • Zidovudine + ribavirin – antagonism in vitro
  • Tenofovir + NSAIDs – increased risk of nephrotoxicity
  • Abacavir + alcohol – increases abacavir levels
