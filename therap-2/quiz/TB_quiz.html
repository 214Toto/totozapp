Scenario 1 Management of Active TB & Drug Interactions

A 38-year-old male patient diagnosed with pulmonary tuberculosis is started on standard quadruple therapy (Isoniazid, Rifampicin, Pyrazinamide, and Ethambutol). His past medical history is significant for generalized tonic-clonic seizures, well-controlled on Phenytoin for 5 years. Two weeks after starting anti-TB treatment, he experiences slurred speech, ataxia, nystagmus, and confusion. Laboratory evaluation reveals normal renal function but elevated plasma phenytoin levels.
1.1 Which anti-TB drug is responsible for the elevated phenytoin levels, and what is its specific mechanism of interaction?
1.2 Explain the mechanism of action of Isoniazid and state why it is particularly potent against Mycobacterium tuberculosis.
1.3 How does Rifampicin interact with cytochrome P450 enzymes, and how does its metabolic effect compare to Isoniazid's effect?
1.4 Name the adverse effect of Isoniazid that affects peripheral nerves, explain its underlying biochemical mechanism, and describe how it is prevented.
1.5 Contrast the metabolic fate of Isoniazid in "fast acetylators" versus "slow acetylators" regarding toxicity risks.
Click to reveal model answers

Model Answers

1.1 — Isoniazid (INH). INH inhibits Cytochrome P450 enzymes in the liver, thereby slowing the hepatic metabolism of phenytoin and causing toxic accumulation.
1.2 — INH inhibits enoyl-acyl carrier protein reductase, blocking the synthesis of mycolic acids required for the mycobacterial cell wall. Because mycolic acids are unique to mycobacteria, INH exhibits selective toxicity and potent tuberculocidal activity against rapidly multiplying bacilli.
1.3 — Rifampicin is a potent enzyme inducer of Cytochrome P450, which accelerates the clearance of coadministered drugs. This opposes INH's enzyme inhibitory effect, though drug interactions must be evaluated based on net patient response and drug monitoring.
1.4 — Peripheral neuritis. INH inhibits pyridoxal kinase and increases renal excretion of pyridoxine (vitamin B6), leading to B6 deficiency. It is prevented by prophylactic co-administration of oral pyridoxine.
1.5 — Fast acetylators rapidly convert INH into acetylisoniazid (acetylhydrazine), putting them at higher risk for hepatitis. Slow acetylators maintain higher levels of parent INH and excreted metabolites, putting them at higher risk for peripheral neuropathy.

Scenario 2 Renal Failure & TB Treatment

A 60-year-old female with chronic kidney disease (CrCl < 10 mL/min) is started on anti-TB therapy for pulmonary tuberculosis. Three weeks into treatment, she complains of blurred vision and difficulty distinguishing red from green. She also develops acute pain and swelling in her wrists and knees.
2.1 Identify the anti-TB drug causing her visual disturbances, describe the specific condition, and state why renal failure exacerbated this condition.
2.2 What exact mechanism of action does Ethambutol utilize to inhibit mycobacteria?
2.3 Which two drugs in her regimen are responsible for her joint pain and swelling, and what is the underlying physiological mechanism?
2.4 Describe the specific site of action of Pyrazinamide and explain why it is inactive against extracellular tubercle bacilli at neutral pH.
2.5 What specific dosage adjustment is required for Ethambutol in this patient to prevent further toxicity?
Click to reveal model answers

Model Answers

2.1 — Ethambutol causes optic (retrobulbar) neuritis (loss of visual acuity, red-green color blindness). Since 50% of ethambutol is excreted unchanged by the kidneys, renal failure leads to drug accumulation and heightened toxicity if doses are not reduced.
2.2 — Ethambutol inhibits arabinosyl transferase enzymes, thereby blocking the incorporation of mycolic acids into the arabinogalactan layer of the mycobacterial cell wall.
2.3 — Pyrazinamide and Ethambutol. Both drugs inhibit the renal tubular clearance of uric acid, causing hyperuricemia and secondary gout arthritis / arthralgia.
2.4 — Pyrazinamide acts on intracellular bacilli within macrophage phagosomes. It requires an acidic pH (~5.0) to exert its tuberculocidal effect; at neutral pH (extracellular space), it is completely inactive.
2.5 — In patients with creatinine clearance < 10 mL/min, the dose of Ethambutol must be reduced by 50% (or the dosing interval extended).

Scenario 3 MDR-TB Management

A 45-year-old patient with Multi-Drug Resistant Tuberculosis (MDR-TB) is started on a second-line regimen containing Bedaquiline, Delamanid, Moxifloxacin, Amikacin, and Cycloserine. Two months into therapy, the patient reports hearing loss and ECG shows QT prolongation.
3.1 Define Multi-Drug Resistant Tuberculosis (MDR-TB) and list three general clinical indications for utilizing second-line anti-TB drugs.
3.2 Describe the mechanism of action of Bedaquiline and state which drugs in this regimen contribute to the observed QT prolongation.
3.3 Identify the drug causing the patient's high-frequency hearing loss, state its class, and list its two major characteristic organ toxicities.
3.4 Describe the mechanism of action of Delamanid in the treatment of MDR-TB.
3.5 What is the mechanism of action of Fluoroquinolones (e.g., Moxifloxacin) against M. tuberculosis?
Click to reveal model answers

Model Answers

3.1 — MDR-TB is defined as tuberculosis resistant to at least Isoniazid and Rifampicin. Second-line drugs are indicated for: (1) Resistance to first-line agents, (2) Failure of clinical response to first-line therapy, and (3) Serious treatment-limiting adverse drug reactions to first-line drugs.
3.2 — Bedaquiline inhibits mycobacterial ATP synthase, blocking energy production. Bedaquiline, Delamanid, and Moxifloxacin (a fluoroquinolone) all carry risks of extending cardiac repolarization, synergistically causing QT prolongation.
3.3 — Amikacin (an aminoglycoside). Its major toxicities are ototoxicity (auditory and vestibular damage) and nephrotoxicity.
3.4 — Delamanid inhibits the synthesis of mycolic acids, destabilizing and compromising the integrity of the mycobacterial cell wall.
3.5 — Fluoroquinolones inhibit bacterial DNA gyrase (topoisomerase II) and topoisomerase IV, preventing DNA supercoiling, replication, and transcription.

Scenario 4 Leprosy Management

A 52-year-old patient with lepromatous leprosy is started on multi-drug therapy (MDT) consisting of Dapsone, Rifampicin, and Clofazimine. Two weeks later, he develops dark urine, pallor, and jaundice. Laboratory studies show hemolytic anemia with Heinz bodies. He later develops tender erythematous nodules on his extremities.
4.1 What is the mechanism of action of Dapsone, and what pharmacological classification describes its effect on M. leprae?
4.2 Explain the cause of the patient's anemia and Heinz bodies in the context of Dapsone therapy.
4.3 Identify the inflammatory reaction the patient experienced (Erythema Nodosum Leprosum) and state two pharmacological agents used to suppress it.
4.4 Describe the primary mechanism of action of Clofazimine and list two non-dermatologic / dermatologic side effects associated with it.
4.5 State the three main objectives recommended by WHO for utilizing combination multi-drug therapy (MDT) in leprosy treatment.
Click to reveal model answers

Model Answers

4.1 — Dapsone inhibits dihydroperonate synthetase, blocking folic acid synthesis. It is classified pharmacologically as a leprostatic agent.
4.2 — Dapsone causes oxidative denaturation of hemoglobin (forming Heinz bodies) and hemolysis, which is especially severe in patients with G6PD deficiency.
4.3 — Erythema Nodosum Leprosum (ENL / Type 2 lepra reaction). It is suppressed using Corticosteroids or Thalidomide. (Clofazimine also possesses anti-inflammatory action that helps suppress lepra reactions).
4.4 — Clofazimine binds selectively to guanine bases of bacterial DNA, blocking template function; it also activates phospholipase A2 to accumulate toxic lysophospholipids. Side effects include reddish-black skin discoloration, dryness, pruritus, and photodermatitis.
4.5 — The three main objectives: (1) Eliminate persister bacilli, (2) Prevent the emergence of drug resistance, and (3) Reduce the overall duration of therapy.
