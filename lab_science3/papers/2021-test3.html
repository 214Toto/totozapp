1. Describe the different forms of intracranial hemorrhage. For any one form of hemorrhage for which Cerebral Spinal Fluid examination is useful in its diagnosis, describe the CSF findings. [10 Marks]

Model Answer

Forms of intracranial hemorrhage:

  • Intracerebral hemorrhage: Bleeding within brain parenchyma (hypertensive, amyloid angiopathy).
  • Subarachnoid hemorrhage: Bleeding into subarachnoid space (ruptured aneurysm).
  • Subdural hemorrhage: Between dura and arachnoid (traumatic).
  • Epidural hemorrhage: Between skull and dura (middle meningeal artery tear).
  • Intraventricular hemorrhage: Bleeding into ventricles (often extension).

CSF findings in subarachnoid hemorrhage:

  • Opening pressure: Elevated (>200 mmH₂O).
  • Appearance: Bloody or xanthochromic (yellow supernatant after centrifugation).
  • Cell count: High RBC count (no decrease from tube 1 to tube 4).
  • Protein: Elevated due to blood.
  • Glucose: Normal (unless infection).

2. Patient presents with splenomegaly and you suspect it might be due to a parasitic infection. By logical sequence, which laboratory investigations would you as a doctor request? Give reasons for your selection of specific laboratory investigation. [10 Marks]

Model Answer

Stepwise investigations for parasitic splenomegaly:

  1. Full blood count & peripheral smear: To detect anemia, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia; and to look for malaria parasites, Leishmania (if seen in monocytes). Reason: Malaria and visceral leishmaniasis are common causes.
  2. Thick and thin blood films (for malaria): To detect Plasmodium species. Reason: Malaria is endemic and a major cause.
  3. Serology for malaria antibodies (if films negative): To detect hyper-reactive malarial splenomegaly (HMS). Reason: High IgM titres.
  4. Leishmania serology (DAT or rK39): If visceral leishmaniasis suspected. Reason: To detect anti-Leishmania antibodies.
  5. Bone marrow aspirate or splenic aspirate: To look for Leishmania amastigotes (if serology positive or high suspicion). Reason: Definitive diagnosis for visceral leishmaniasis.
  6. Abdominal ultrasound: To assess splenic size and rule out other causes (e.g., portal hypertension). Reason: Non-invasive imaging.
  7. HIV test: Since parasitic infections may be opportunistic. Reason: Immunosuppression can alter presentation.

3. Patient presents with cervical lymphadenopathy and you suspect it might be due to a non-infectious cause. List the possible causes. Which investigations would you request to make a diagnosis of the cause and give reasons for the selection of each investigation? [10 Marks]

Model Answer

Possible non-infectious causes of cervical lymphadenopathy:

  • Lymphoma (Hodgkin's and non-Hodgkin's)
  • Metastatic carcinoma (head and neck, lung, breast)
  • Sarcoidosis
  • Autoimmune diseases (SLE, rheumatoid arthritis)
  • Drug-induced (e.g., phenytoin)
  • Kawasaki disease
  • Castleman disease

Investigations and reasons:

  • Excisional lymph node biopsy: Gold standard for histopathology and immunohistochemistry to differentiate lymphoma, metastases, etc. Reason: Definitive diagnosis.
  • Full blood count & peripheral smear: To look for leukemic cells, cytopenias. Reason: May suggest hematological malignancy.
  • ESR & CRP: Inflammatory markers; elevated in sarcoidosis, autoimmune, malignancy. Reason: Supportive.
  • Chest X-ray or CT chest: To detect mediastinal lymphadenopathy (sarcoidosis, lymphoma). Reason: Staging and differential.
  • Serum LDH: Elevated in lymphoma and metastatic disease. Reason: Tumor marker.
  • Serum ACE: Elevated in sarcoidosis. Reason: Specific for sarcoidosis.
  • Autoimmune screen (ANA, RF): For connective tissue diseases. Reason: To rule out autoimmune.
  • HIV serology: HIV can cause generalized lymphadenopathy and predispose to malignancies. Reason: Important differential.

4. Discuss step by step the procedures for Laboratory Viral CNS diagnosis in a Known specimen with Herpes encephalitis. [10 Marks]

Model Answer

Stepwise laboratory diagnosis of Herpes encephalitis (HSV):

  1. Specimen collection: CSF collected via lumbar puncture (at least 2-3 mL in sterile tubes). Blood for serology.
  2. CSF analysis:
    • Physical appearance: Usually clear or slightly turbid.
    • Cell count & differential: Lymphocytic pleocytosis (elevated WBC with lymphocyte predominance).
    • Protein: Elevated.
    • Glucose: Normal or slightly decreased.
  3. Molecular detection:
    • PCR for HSV DNA: Gold standard – highly sensitive and specific; detects HSV-1 and HSV-2. Reason: Rapid and definitive.
  4. Serology: Detect HSV IgM and IgG in serum/CSF (intrathecal antibody synthesis). Reason: Supportive, but less sensitive early.
  5. Viral culture: Inoculate CSF onto cell culture (Vero cells) – but less sensitive and takes days. Reason: Confirmatory but slow.
  6. Antigen detection: Immunofluorescence or ELISA for HSV antigens in CSF – less sensitive than PCR.
  7. Brain biopsy (rare): If diagnosis uncertain, but invasive.

Interpretation: Positive PCR in CSF confirms HSV encephalitis. Negative PCR does not exclude early infection; repeat testing may be needed.

5. Write short notes on the features and laboratory identification of Neisseria meningitidis. [10 Marks]

Model Answer

Features:

  • Morphology: Gram-negative diplococci (kidney-bean shaped), capsulated, oxidase-positive, catalase-positive.
  • Clinical features: Meningitis (fever, headache, neck stiffness), meningococcemia (petechial rash, sepsis), Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome (adrenal hemorrhage).
  • Transmission: Respiratory droplets; nasopharyngeal carriage.

Laboratory identification:

  • CSF examination: Turbid, elevated WBC (polymorphs), elevated protein, low glucose.
  • Gram stain of CSF: Intracellular Gram-negative diplococci.
  • Culture: CSF, blood, or skin lesion aspirates on chocolate agar or blood agar with CO₂; colonies are oxidase-positive, catalase-positive, and produce acid from glucose and maltose.
  • Antigen detection: Latex agglutination detects capsular polysaccharide in CSF (rapid).
  • PCR: Detects meningococcal DNA in CSF (sensitive and rapid).
  • Serotyping: Based on capsular polysaccharide (serogroups A, B, C, etc.).
  • Antimicrobial susceptibility testing: Penicillin susceptibility (some strains resistant).
