Q1 Question 1

ADA deficiency is an inherited disorder that damages the immune system and causes severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID). Patients lack immune protection against bacteria, viruses, and fungi, and are prone to recurrent opportunistic infections. Affected children also grow slowly and some have developmental delay.

a What is the normal reaction catalyzed by ADA, and what is the metabolic defect in this condition? (1 mark)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 1 mark

ADA catalyzes the deamination of adenosine → inosine and deoxyadenosine → deoxyinosine. The defect is loss of this enzyme activity.

b Which metabolites accumulate in ADA deficiency? (1 mark)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 1 mark

Adenosine and deoxyadenosine (and consequently dATP) accumulate.

c What is the final catabolic product of adenosine in the normal body? (1 mark)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 1 mark

Uric acid.

d Sketch the outline of adenosine degradation in the body. (2 marks)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 2 marks

AMP → Adenosine → (ADA) → Inosine → Hypoxanthine → (Xanthine Oxidase) → Xanthine → (Xanthine Oxidase) → Uric Acid.

e Which tissues have the highest ADA activity? (1 mark)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 1 mark

Lymphoid tissue (lymphocytes, thymus, spleen).

f Explain the biochemical basis of immune system damage in ADA deficiency. (1 mark)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 1 mark

Accumulated dATP inhibits ribonucleotide reductase, blocking DNA synthesis/repair and causing lymphotoxicity (killing T and B lymphocytes).

g Explain the biochemical basis of developmental delay in ADA deficiency. (1 mark)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 1 mark

Systemic toxicity from purine metabolite accumulation, combined with chronic recurrent infections, leads to energy failure and impaired cellular development.

Q2 Question 2

A 56-year-old male presented with intense redness, heat, and pain over his right great toe at the metatarsophalangeal joint. Joint fluid showed birefringent crystals. He was treated with allopurinol and advised to go vegan.

a What is the most likely diagnosis? (1 mark)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 1 mark

Gout.

b This disease results from excessive degradation of which class of metabolites? (1 mark)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 1 mark

Purines (purine nucleotides).

c What metabolite forms the birefringent crystals seen in the joint fluid? (1 mark)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 1 mark

Monosodium urate (urate crystals).

d What is the normal serum range for this metabolite? (1 mark)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 1 mark

3.5 – 7.0 mg/dL (or 4–7 mg/100 mL).

e Name two enzyme defects that can cause this condition. (1 mark)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 1 mark

  • PRPP synthetase overactivity.
  • HGPRT deficiency (Lesch-Nyhan syndrome).
  • Glutamine PRPP amidotransferase overactivity.
f Sugary foods worsen gout. Explain the biochemical basis. (2 marks)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 2 marks

Fructose phosphorylation by fructokinase rapidly depletes ATP, generating AMP/ADP. This drives purine nucleotide degradation and increases uric acid production.

g List two other factors that promote gout. (1 mark)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 1 mark

  • Alcohol consumption.
  • High-purine diet (red meat, seafood).
  • Dehydration or diuretic use.
h What is the mechanism of action of allopurinol? (1 mark)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 1 mark

Allopurinol competitively (suicide) inhibits xanthine oxidase, blocking conversion of hypoxanthine and xanthine to uric acid.

i Why was the patient advised to go vegan? (1 mark)
Click to see model answer

Model Answer 1 mark

A plant-based diet reduces exogenous purine intake (avoiding purine-rich meat and seafood), lowering serum uric acid levels.
