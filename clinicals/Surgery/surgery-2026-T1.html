Q1 Image Interpretation & Management

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

1. Image Description: Erect abdominal plain X-ray showing multiple dilated loops of small bowel with prominent valvulae conniventes. Multiple step-ladder air-fluid levels (white arrows), and a notable absence of gas in the distal colon/rectum — highly indicative of acute mechanical intestinal obstruction.

2. Five Causes:

  • Post-operative intra-abdominal adhesions (most common).
  • Obstructed/incarcerated hernias (inguinal, femoral, umbilical).
  • Malignancy (colorectal carcinoma or small bowel tumours).
  • Volvulus (sigmoid or cecal).
  • Intussusception or inflammatory conditions (e.g. Crohn's disease).

3. Pathophysiology of Air-Fluid Levels: Mechanical obstruction blocks peristalsis. Proximal bowel accumulates swallowed air and GI secretions. Gas separates from liquid, forming horizontal air-fluid levels on erect radiograph.

4. Normal Air-Fluid Levels: Up to 2–3 fluid levels in a healthy adult, typically in the stomach and cecum/hepatic flexure.

5. Initial Management:

  • Fluid Resuscitation: Large-bore IV access, isotonic crystalloids.
  • GI Decompression: Nasogastric tube on low intermittent suction.
  • NPO: Strict nil per os.
  • Monitoring: Foley catheter for hourly urine output.
  • Analgesia & Labs: IV analgesics, FBC, U&E, type & screen.

Q2 Breast Lump & Triple Assessment

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

1. History Questions:

  • When did you first notice the lump, and has it changed in size/shape/consistency?
  • Any localized pain, nipple discharge (bloody/unilateral), or skin changes?
  • Menstrual history (menarche, menopause) and obstetric history (age at first pregnancy, breastfeeding)?
  • Personal/family history of breast or ovarian cancer?
  • Current or past hormonal replacement therapy or oral contraceptives?

2. Examination Findings:

  • Inspection: Asymmetry, skin dimpling, nipple retraction/inversion/ulceration, or peau d'orange (right breast).
  • Palpation: Location (quadrant), size, margins, consistency, mobility relative to skin/pectoralis.
  • Lymph nodes: Axillary, supraclavicular, infraclavicular.

3. Differential Diagnosis:

  • Breast Carcinoma (Infiltrating Ductal): Primary suspect given age (55, postmenopausal) and peau d'orange.
  • Fibroadenoma or Phylloides tumour.
  • Breast abscess or fat necrosis.

4. Next Steps — Triple Assessment:

  • Clinical Examination: Detailed documentation.
  • Diagnostic Imaging: Bilateral mammogram + targeted breast ultrasound.
  • Tissue Biopsy: Core Needle Biopsy for histology and receptor status (ER, PR, HER2).

Q3 Inguinal Hernia Anatomy

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

1. Spot Diagnosis: Right Inguinal Hernia (inguinoscrotal swelling).

2. Three Differentials:

  • Hydrocele (fluid in tunica vaginalis).
  • Varicocele ("bag of worms" pampiniform plexus).
  • Spermatocele / epididymal cyst.

3. Definition of Inguinal Canal: An oblique, intermuscular tunnel ~4 cm in the lower anterior abdominal wall, running downwards, forwards, and medially, connecting the deep and superficial inguinal rings.

4. Anatomical Points:

  • Mid-Inguinal Point: Midway between ASIS and pubic symphysis; marks deep ring and femoral artery.
  • Midpoint of Inguinal Ligament: Centre between ASIS and pubic tubercle.

5. Contents in Males: Spermatic cord (vas deferens, testicular artery, pampiniform plexus, cremasteric artery, artery to vas, genital branch of genitofemoral nerve, lymphatics) + ilioinguinal nerve.

6. Boundaries:

  • Anterior: External oblique aponeurosis, reinforced laterally by internal oblique.
  • Posterior: Transversalis fascia, reinforced medially by conjoint tendon.
  • Roof: Arched fibres of internal oblique and transversus abdominis.
  • Floor: Inguinal ligament, reinforced medially by lacunar ligament.

Q4 Head Injury & GCS

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

1. CT Description: Non-contrast axial brain CT shows a large, hyperdense (acute blood), crescent-shaped/concave extra-axial collection along the left frontoparietal convexity. Significant mass effect, effacement of sulci, compression of the left lateral ventricle, and rightward midline shift.

2. Diagnosis: Acute Subdural Haematoma (SDH).

3. GCS Calculation:

  • Eye Opening: to pain = 2
  • Verbal: inappropriate = 3
  • Motor: withdraws to pain = 4
  • Total: 2 + 3 + 4 = 9/15 → Moderate Head Injury

4. Secondary Brain Injury Examples:

  • Cerebral ischaemia / hypoxia.
  • Intracranial hypertension.
  • Cerebral oedema.
  • Systemic hypotension / hypercapnia.

5. Vulnerable Area & Vessel: The Pterion (junction of frontal, parietal, temporal, sphenoid). The vessel is the Middle Meningeal Artery (though acute SDH originates from ruptured cortical bridging veins).

Q5 Burns & Resuscitation

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

1. Description: Female patient with severe facial oedema, blistering of the lips, extensive partial-to-full thickness burns involving perioral and perinasal regions, and soot/carbonaceous singeing around nose and mouth — suggestive of flash or thermal inhalation injury.

2. Emergency History Questions:

  • Did the injury occur in an enclosed space or outdoors?
  • Was there an explosion, toxic exposure, or loss of consciousness?
  • Time of incident (for fluid resuscitation calculation).
  • Any difficulty breathing, hoarseness, or sore throat?
  • Past medical history, medications, tetanus status.

3. ER Assessment Features:

  • Airway: Stridor, hoarseness, accessory muscle use, soot in pharynx.
  • Breathing: RR, SpO₂, signs of CO poisoning or inhalation injury.
  • Circulation: Large-bore IV lines, haemodynamic stability.
  • Burn Survey: TBSA calculation and depth assessment.

4. Burn Percentage: Head and neck = 9% TBSA (anterior face/neck ≈ 4.5%).

5. Admission Criteria:

  • Partial-thickness burns > 10% TBSA.
  • Burns to face, hands, feet, genitalia, perineum, or major joints.
  • Third-degree burns in any age group.
  • Suspected inhalation injury.
  • Electrical/chemical burns, comorbidities, or concomitant trauma.

Q6 Thyroid Goitre

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

1. Two Differentials:

  • Simple Endemic Goitre (Multinodular Goitre).
  • Thyroid Neoplasm (Benign Adenoma or Carcinoma).

2. Investigations:

  • Lab 1: Thyroid Function Tests (T3, T4, TSH).
  • Lab 2: Full Blood Count.
  • Lab 3: Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology (FNAC).
  • Imaging: Thyroid Ultrasound (or non-contrast CT neck for retrosternal extension).

3. Likely Causes:

  • Dietary iodine deficiency (most common globally).
  • Goitrogen consumption (foods/medications interfering with hormone synthesis).
  • Genetic biosynthetic defects or autoimmune thyroiditis.

4. Intra-operative Complications:

  • Primary haemorrhage from highly vascular vessels.
  • Recurrent laryngeal nerve injury (hoarseness).
  • Tracheomalacia or parathyroid damage.

5. Long-term Complication: Permanent hypothyroidism requiring life-long levothyroxine.

Q7 Parotid Tumour

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

1. Diagnosis: Parotid Gland Tumour (e.g. Pleomorphic Adenoma vs. Parotid Carcinoma).

2. Depth Feature: Mobility of the mass during jaw clenching — fixation to deep masseter or mandible indicates deep-lobe invasion.

3. Malignant Features:

  • Rapid growth.
  • Ipsilateral Facial Nerve (CN VII) palsy/weakness.
  • Firm-to-hard consistency with fixation to surrounding tissues/skin.
  • Palpable cervical lymphadenopathy.

4. Most Important Investigation: Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology (FNAC).

5. Innervation: Postganglionic parasympathetic fibres from the Auriculotemporal Nerve (Otic ganglion / Glossopharyngeal CN IX). Sensory from Auriculotemporal and Great Auricular nerves.

Q8 Ureteric Obstruction

Model Answer

The arrow points to a filling defect or cutoff at the lower right ureter near the vesicoureteral junction.

2. Two Abnormalities:

  • Unilateral right-sided hydroureteronephrosis.
  • Delayed/obstructed contrast clearance from the right collecting system.

3. Next Steps:

  • Non-contrast CT Abdomen/Pelvis (CT Urogram) to identify the obstruction (calculus, stricture, or extrinsic compression).
  • Urgent decompression via retrograde ureteral stenting (Double-J) or percutaneous nephrostomy (PCN) if infection or renal impairment is present.

4. Possible Complications:

  • Pyelonephritis / urosepsis.
  • Permanent renal atrophy (obstructive nephropathy).
  • Renal calculus formation from chronic stasis.

Q9 Urinary Catheterisation

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

1. Image Description: Multiple soft, sterile latex or silicone indwelling urinary catheters (Foley catheters) with inflating valve ports and retention balloon channels.

2. Three Clinical Indications:

  • Acute/chronic urinary retention (e.g., BPH).
  • Accurate hourly urine output monitoring in critically ill patients.
  • Perioperative bladder decompression during urological/pelvic/general surgery.

3. Insertion Steps in Males:

  • Position supine, perform hand hygiene, open tray, don sterile gloves.
  • Drape, clean penis/glans with antiseptic, retract prepuce if uncircumcised.
  • Instil sterile anaesthetic lubricant (2% lidocaine) into urethra, hold meatus closed for efficacy.
  • Hold penis vertically at 90° to straighten urethra, advance catheter to the hub.
  • Confirm urine flow, inflate balloon with sterile water, gently pull back, advance slightly, secure, return foreskin.

4. Three Complications:

  • Catheter-Associated Urinary Tract Infection (CAUTI).
  • Urethral trauma / false passage (bleeding, strictures).
  • Paraphimosis (failure to return foreskin).

5. Key Care Points:

  • Maintain a closed drainage system; bag below bladder level.
  • Daily meatal hygiene with soap and water.
  • Monitor for systemic infection (fever, chills) and inspect urine colour/clarity/volume regularly.
