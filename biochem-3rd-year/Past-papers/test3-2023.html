Q1 Gout & Purine Metabolism

A 56-year-old male patient presented with intense redness, heat, and pain over his right great toe at the metatarsophalangeal joint. Fluid from his joint showed birefringent crystals. The patient was treated with allopurinol and was also advised to go vegan.

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

(a) Gout.

(b) Purines (adenine and guanine).

(c) Urate crystals (monosodium urate).

(d) Normal serum uric acid: 3.4 – 7.0 mg/dL (in men); 2.4 – 6.0 mg/dL (in women).

(e) Two enzyme defects:
1. HGPRTase deficiency (Lesch-Nyhan syndrome).
2. PRPP synthetase overactivity (increased PRPP production).

(f) Fructose-rich foods (sugary items) cause rapid ATP depletion → ADP accumulation → AMP formation → AMP deamination to inosine and hypoxanthine, which are converted to uric acid, increasing its production.

(g) Two other factors:
1. High purine diet (organ meats, seafood).
2. Alcohol intake (especially beer).

(h) Allopurinol competitively inhibits xanthine oxidase, decreasing uric acid formation.

(i) A vegan diet reduces intake of purine-rich animal products and also lowers fructose intake, thereby decreasing uric acid production.

Q2 Replication Fork Diagram

Based on the following diagram of replication with labels 1 to 12 answer the questions below.

(Replication fork diagram with labels 1–12)

Model Answer

(a) Label 1 = Replication fork. There are two replication forks in a replication bubble.

(b) Label 2 = DNA polymerase. It synthesizes new DNA strands by adding nucleotides.

(c) Label 3 = Topoisomerase. There are two types: Topoisomerase I (relaxes supercoils) and Topoisomerase II (cuts and rejoins DNA).

(d) Label 4 = Leading strand; Label 11 = Lagging strand.

(e) Label 5 = Helicase. It unwinds the DNA double helix by breaking hydrogen bonds.

(f) Label 6 = Single-strand binding proteins (SSB). They stabilize single-stranded DNA and prevent reannealing.

(g) Label 7 = Primase. In prokaryotes, it synthesizes RNA primers; in eukaryotes, it is part of the primosome complex.

(h) Label 8 = 3'-OH end. It provides the free hydroxyl group for DNA polymerase to add nucleotides.

(i) Label 9 = Okazaki fragments. In prokaryotes they are longer (~1000–2000 nt), in eukaryotes they are shorter (~100–200 nt).

(j) Label 10 = DNA polymerase I (in prokaryotes). It removes RNA primers and replaces them with DNA, and also fills gaps.

Q4 lac Operon

The lac operon (diagram below) is a portion of the bacterial chromosome that controls the synthesis of three enzymes involved in the metabolism of lactose.

(lac operon diagram with regulator, promoter, operator, structural genes)

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

(a)
Regulator gene (lacI): Codes for the repressor protein.
Promoter (P): Binding site for RNA polymerase.
Operator (O): Binding site for the repressor; controls access of RNA polymerase to structural genes.
Structural genes (lacZ, lacY, lacA): Encode β-galactosidase, permease, and transacetylase.

(b) Repression: In absence of inducer, repressor binds to operator, preventing RNA polymerase from transcribing structural genes.

(c) Derepression: Inducer (allolactose) binds to repressor, causing conformational change that releases repressor from operator, allowing transcription.

(d) Positive regulation: When glucose is low, cAMP levels rise; cAMP binds to CAP, and CAP-cAMP complex binds to CAP site near promoter, enhancing RNA polymerase binding and transcription.

(e) High glucose: Lowers cAMP, so CAP-cAMP complex does not form; RNA polymerase binds poorly, reducing transcription even in presence of lactose (catabolite repression).

Q5 DNA Analysis Techniques

In the future, doctors may be able to evaluate your DNA to check for genetic markers related to specific medical conditions. Your personalized genetic profile could be kept on record and referred to in order to develop personal medical treatment based on your unique DNA sequencing.

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

(a) Sanger method uses dideoxynucleotides (ddNTPs) to terminate DNA synthesis. Four reactions (each with a different ddNTP) produce fragments of different lengths, which are separated by electrophoresis to read the sequence.

(b) Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH).

(c) Southern blotting or PCR-based techniques (e.g., allele-specific PCR).

(d) PCR steps:
i. Denaturation (95°C) – separate strands.
ii. Annealing (55–65°C) – primers bind.
iii. Extension (72°C) – Taq polymerase extends primers.

(e)
i. Isoschizomer: Different restriction enzymes that recognize the same sequence and cut at the same position.
ii. Neoschizomer: Enzymes that recognize the same sequence but cut at different positions.
iii. Isocaudomer: Enzymes that recognize different sequences but generate compatible cohesive ends.

(f) Electrophoresis separates DNA fragments based on size and charge. Negatively charged DNA moves toward the positive electrode; smaller fragments migrate faster through the gel matrix.

(g) Three types of probes:
1. Radioactive probes (³²P-labeled).
2. Fluorescent probes (e.g., fluorophore-labeled).
3. Chemiluminescent probes (e.g., digoxigenin-labeled).
