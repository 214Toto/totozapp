Q35 Differentiate DNA fingerprinting from DNA footprinting. (2 marks)

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

DNA Fingerprinting: Technique to identify individuals based on unique patterns of tandem repeats (VNTRs) in their DNA. Used in forensics and paternity testing.

DNA Footprinting: Method to determine the precise binding site of a protein on DNA by protecting the DNA from cleavage at the binding site. Used to study protein-DNA interactions.

Q36 What are the different types of enzymes and proteins required for replication and what are their functions? (3 marks)

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

  • Helicase: Unwinds DNA double helix.
  • Topoisomerase: Relieves supercoiling.
  • Primase: Synthesizes RNA primers.
  • DNA polymerase III: Elongates new DNA strands (prokaryotes).
  • DNA polymerase I: Removes RNA primers and replaces with DNA.
  • DNA ligase: Joins Okazaki fragments.
  • Single-strand binding proteins: Stabilize single-stranded DNA.

Q37 What are the main causes of damage to DNA and how are they repaired? (3 marks)

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

Causes:

  • Ultraviolet (UV) light → pyrimidine dimers.
  • Chemical mutagens (e.g., alkylating agents).
  • Oxidative damage (reactive oxygen species).
  • Spontaneous deamination or depurination.

Repair mechanisms:

  • Nucleotide excision repair (NER): Removes bulky lesions like thymine dimers.
  • Base excision repair (BER): Corrects single-base damage.
  • Mismatch repair: Corrects replication errors.
  • Non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) / homologous recombination: Fixes double-strand breaks.

Q38 How is hnRNA converted to mRNA? (3 marks)

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

  • 5' capping: Addition of 7-methylguanosine cap to the 5' end.
  • Polyadenylation: Addition of a poly(A) tail to the 3' end.
  • Splicing: Removal of introns and joining of exons by the spliceosome.
  • These modifications protect mRNA, aid in export, and enhance translation.

Q39 Define the terms: (a) Transition and transversion (b) Introns and exons (c) Karyotype and Phenotype (1+1+1 marks)

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

(a) Transition: Substitution of a purine with another purine (A↔G) or pyrimidine with another pyrimidine (C↔T). Transversion: Substitution of a purine with a pyrimidine or vice versa.

(b) Introns: Non-coding sequences within a gene that are spliced out during mRNA processing. Exons: Coding sequences that are retained in mature mRNA.

(c) Karyotype: The complete set of chromosomes in a cell, arranged by size and morphology. Phenotype: The observable physical or biochemical characteristics of an organism, determined by genotype and environment.

Q40 Define the terms: (a) Totipotency and Pluripotency (b) Chimeric DNA and vector (c) Isoschizomers and Neoschizomers (1+1+1 marks)

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

(a) Totipotency: Ability of a single cell to develop into a complete organism. Pluripotency: Ability of a cell to differentiate into any cell type of the body (but not extra-embryonic tissues).

(b) Chimeric DNA: Recombinant DNA formed by joining sequences from different organisms. Vector: A DNA molecule (e.g., plasmid) used to carry foreign DNA into a host cell.

(c) Isoschizomers: Restriction enzymes that recognize the same DNA sequence and cut at the same position. Neoschizomers: Enzymes that recognize the same sequence but cut at a different position.
