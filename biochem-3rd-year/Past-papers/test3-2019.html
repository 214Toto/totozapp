Model Answer

(a) No — DNA synthesis requires all four dNTPs. If one is omitted, chain elongation stops; no radioactivity would be incorporated into the acid-precipitable fraction.

(b) Yes — if only dTTPs are labeled with ³²P in the α-phosphate, the label is incorporated into the DNA backbone during polymerization. Other dNTPs would still allow chain elongation, and the newly synthesized DNA would contain radioactive thymidine residues.

(c) No — DNA polymerase incorporates only the α-phosphate of the incoming dNTP into the growing chain. The β and γ phosphates are cleaved as pyrophosphate (PPi) and are not incorporated into DNA. Thus no radioactivity would be found in the precipitate.

Q42 lac Operon Mutations

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

(a) lac operator deletion (O₁): The repressor can no longer bind to the operator. The lac operon becomes constitutively expressed (always on) — transcription occurs even in the absence of inducer (allolactose).

(b) lacI gene inactivation: A non-functional repressor is produced (or none at all). The operon is constitutively expressed — the structural genes are transcribed continuously regardless of inducer presence.

(c) Promoter mutation at -10 region (Pribnow box): RNA polymerase binds less efficiently. This results in reduced transcription of the structural genes, leading to decreased expression of β-galactosidase, permease, and transacetylase.

Q43 SCIDS & Adenosine Deaminase

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

(a) Adenosine deaminase (ADA).

(b) ADA catalyses the deamination of adenosine to inosine (and deoxyadenosine to deoxyinosine), removing the amino group from the purine base.

(c) ADA deficiency leads to accumulation of deoxyadenosine (dA) and its derivative dATP. dATP allosterically inhibits ribonucleotide reductase — the enzyme that converts ribonucleotides to deoxyribonucleotides. This results in depletion of other dNTPs (dCTP, dGTP, dTTP), impairing DNA synthesis and causing lymphocyte death, manifesting as SCIDS.

Q44 Sulphadoxine & Thymidylate Synthase

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

Thymidylate synthase catalyses the conversion of dUMP to dTMP, a critical step in DNA synthesis. Sulphadoxine is a structural analogue of p-aminobenzoic acid (PABA) and inhibits dihydropteroate synthase in the folate pathway, indirectly affecting thymidylate synthase.

Mutations in the thymidylate synthase gene can alter the enzyme's active site, reducing the drug's binding affinity. This confers resistance to the antimalarial combination (Sulphadoxine-Pyrimethamine) by allowing the parasite to continue dTMP synthesis despite the presence of the drug, thereby maintaining DNA replication and survival.

Q45 Recombinant DNA Technology

Click to reveal model answer

Model Answer

1. Isolation of DNA: Extract genomic DNA from the source organism and plasmid DNA from the vector.

2. Restriction Enzyme Digestion: Cut both the target DNA and the plasmid vector with the same restriction endonuclease to generate complementary sticky ends.

3. Ligation: Mix the digested DNA fragments with the linearized vector in the presence of DNA ligase to form recombinant plasmid DNA.

4. Transformation: Introduce the recombinant plasmid into a host bacterium (e.g., E. coli) via heat shock or electroporation.

5. Selection and Screening: Plate transformed bacteria on selective media (e.g., antibiotic-containing agar) and screen for colonies containing the desired insert (e.g., blue-white screening or PCR).

6. Amplification and Expression: Grow selected colonies in liquid culture to produce large quantities of the recombinant DNA or protein product.
