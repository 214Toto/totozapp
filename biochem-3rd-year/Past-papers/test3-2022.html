Question 1
An 8‑month‑old child is brought to the pediatrician’s office due to excessive sensitivity to the sun. Skin areas exposed to the sun for only a brief period of time were reddened with scaling. Irregular dark spots have also appeared. The pediatrician suspects a genetic disorder, which is related to DNA repair mechanism.
a) What is the most likely DNA damage in this case? (1 mark)
Formation of thymine dimers (cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers) caused by UV radiation.
b) List the major types of DNA damages along with causes for each type of damage. (3 marks)
(i) Thymine dimers – caused by UV light.
(ii) Base excision (oxidative damage, deamination, alkylation) – caused by reactive oxygen species, chemicals.
(iii) Single‑strand breaks – caused by ionizing radiation and chemical toxins.
(iv) Double‑strand breaks – caused by ionizing radiation, chemical toxins.
(v) Mismatched bases – caused by replication errors.
c) List the major DNA repair mechanisms in line with these major types of DNA damages. (3 marks)
(i) Nucleotide excision repair – for thymine dimers and bulky lesions.
(ii) Base excision repair – for oxidative damage, deamination, alkylation.
(iii) Mismatch repair – for replication errors.
(iv) Homologous recombination and Non‑homologous end joining – for double‑strand breaks.
d) Which of these DNA repair mechanisms is most likely be defective in this case? (1 mark)
Nucleotide excision repair (NER) – the mechanism that removes UV‑induced pyrimidine dimers.
e) Name the genetic disorder that might be the suspect of the pediatrician. (2 marks)
Xeroderma pigmentosum (XP) – an autosomal recessive disorder with defective nucleotide excision repair.
Question 2
A patient suffering from chills, vomiting, and cramping was rushed to the emergency department. The physician noted that the patient had eaten wild mushrooms for dinner that he had picked earlier in the day.
a) Identify and name the compound in the wild mushroom that is responsible for the condition of the patient. (1 mark)
α‑Amanitin (found in Amanita phalloides, the death cap mushroom).
b) Which enzyme does this compound inhibit? (1 mark)
RNA polymerase II (and also RNA polymerase III at higher concentrations).
c) How does this compound inhibit (mechanism) the above enzyme? (2 marks)
α‑Amanitin binds to the bridge helix of RNA polymerase II, blocking the translocation step and preventing the addition of nucleotides to the growing RNA chain. This effectively halts transcription.
d) Which major process would have been affected by this enzyme inhibition? (1 mark)
Transcription – the synthesis of mRNA from DNA.
e) What are the three major steps in this process? With a diagram, illustrate each one. (5 marks)
1. Initiation: RNA polymerase binds to the promoter, unwinds DNA, and begins RNA synthesis.
2. Elongation: RNA polymerase moves along the template, adding nucleotides to the 3′ end of the growing RNA chain.
3. Termination: RNA polymerase reaches a termination signal, releases the RNA transcript, and dissociates from DNA.
(Diagram: show promoter, RNA polymerase, template strand, growing RNA, and termination signal.)
Question 3
Your 56‑year‑old male patient presents with intense redness, heat, and pain over his right great toe at the metatarsophalangeal joint. Fluid from this joint shows birefringent crystals.
a) What is the most likely disease in this case? (1 mark)
Gout (acute gouty arthritis).
b) This disease is caused by the degradation of an excessive amount of which of the metabolites? (1 mark)
Purines (adenine and guanine) leading to overproduction of uric acid.
c) Identify the metabolite in the fluid collected from the affected joint that shows as birefringent crystals. (1 mark)
Uric acid (monosodium urate crystals).
d) What is the normal range of this metabolite in the serum? (1 mark)
Normal serum uric acid: 3.4–7.0 mg/dL (men) or 2.4–6.0 mg/dL (women); approximately 200–420 μmol/L.
e) What are the enzyme defects that would cause this condition? (2 marks)
HGPRT deficiency (Lesch‑Nyhan syndrome) – leads to uric acid overproduction.
Overactivity of PRPP synthetase – increases purine synthesis.
Glucose‑6‑phosphatase deficiency (von Gierke's disease) – causes secondary gout.
f) Eating of sugary food items considered to be worsening this condition. Explain it biochemically. (3 marks)
Fructose (from sucrose or high‑fructose corn syrup) is metabolised rapidly, consuming ATP and generating AMP. AMP is degraded to inosine and then to hypoxanthine, which is converted to uric acid. Fructose also stimulates purine nucleotide turnover, leading to increased uric acid production, worsening gout.
g) List two other factors that promote this condition. (1 mark)
Alcohol consumption (especially beer) – increases uric acid production and decreases excretion.
Dehydration – reduces uric acid excretion, increasing serum levels.
Question 4
The lac operon is a portion of the bacterial chromosome that controls the synthesis of three enzymes involved in the metabolism of the sugar lactose.
Regulatory gene  |  Promoter  |  Operator  |  Z  |  Y  |  A
a) Describe the mechanism of repression in lac operon. (2 marks)
In the absence of lactose, the lac repressor (encoded by the lacI gene) binds to the operator sequence. This physically blocks RNA polymerase from transcribing the structural genes (Z, Y, A), so no enzymes for lactose metabolism are produced.
b) Describe the mechanism of derepression in lac operon. (2 marks)
When lactose is present, it is converted to allolactose, which binds to the repressor protein. This causes a conformational change that releases the repressor from the operator. RNA polymerase can then bind the promoter and transcribe the structural genes.
c) Explain the effect of presence of high glucose on the functioning of lac operon. (2 marks)
High glucose decreases cAMP levels (since glucose inhibits adenylate cyclase). cAMP is required to activate CAP (catabolite activator protein). Without the cAMP‑CAP complex binding to the CAP site near the promoter, RNA polymerase binds poorly, so transcription is low even if lactose is present (catabolite repression).
d) Consider E. coli cells, each having one of the following mutations (i to iv). What effect would each mutation have on the function of the lac operon (assuming no glucose is present)?
i. a mutant lac operator that cannot bind repressor: (1 mark)
Constitutive expression – the operon is always transcribed because the repressor cannot bind the operator.
ii. a mutant lac repressor that cannot bind to the lac operator: (1 mark)
Constitutive expression – the repressor cannot bind the operator, so transcription is always on.
iii. a mutant lac repressor that cannot bind to allolactose: (1 mark)
Cannot be induced – the repressor stays bound to the operator even in the presence of lactose, so transcription remains off.
iv. a mutant lac promoter region that cannot bind CAP plus cAMP: (1 mark)
Low transcription – CAP‑cAMP cannot bind to enhance RNA polymerase recruitment, so transcription is reduced even when lactose is present.
Question 5
The following is the sequence of a portion of a bacterial gene (The template strand is on the bottom):
5' GTATCGTTAGCATGCATCGTGAC 3'
3' CATAGCACTAGTACGTCGACTG 5'

Hint: You do not need to know the genetic code and amino acid for respective codon to answer these questions; you just need to know the nature of initiation and termination codons.

a) Assuming that transcription starts with the first T in the template strand, and continues to the end, what would be the sequence of the mRNA derived from this fragment? (2 marks)
5' – AUCGUAUGCACGUACGUAGACUG – 3'
(complement of the template from the first T onward, with U instead of T; antiparallel)
b) Locate and mark the initiation codon and stop codon in this mRNA. (2 marks)
In the mRNA above, the initiation codon is AUG (the first AUG encountered) and the stop codon is UGA (or UAA/UAG) at the end. Mark them clearly in the sequence.
c) Would there be an effect on translation of changing the first G in the template strand to a C? If so, what would be the effect? (2 marks)
Yes. The first G in the template (which pairs with a C in the coding strand) would change the corresponding codon in the mRNA. If this change creates a stop codon or alters the start codon, translation would be prematurely terminated or not initiated. Otherwise it would cause a missense mutation.
d) Would there be an effect on translation of changing the second T in the template strand to a G? If so, what would be the effect? (2 marks)
Yes. The second T in the template would change the complementary base in the mRNA. This could alter a codon, possibly changing the amino acid (missense) or creating a stop codon (nonsense), or if it affects the reading frame (if not a multiple of 3) it could cause a frameshift.
e) Would there be an effect on translation of changing the last T in the template strand to a C? If so, what would be the effect? (2 marks)
Yes. Changing the last T in the template would alter the corresponding codon in the mRNA. If this codon is the stop codon, it could be changed to a sense codon, causing read‑through and a longer protein. If it is a sense codon, it could cause a missense or nonsense mutation.
