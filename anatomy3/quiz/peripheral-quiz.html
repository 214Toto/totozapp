Set 1 Concerning Spinal Nerves and Roots

(a) Spinal nerves emerge through intervertebral foramina.
(b) There are 12 pairs of cervical spinal nerves.

(d) Ventral roots contain motor fibers arising from the anterior gray column of the spinal cord.

Set 2 Regarding Spinal Nerve Exit and Rami

(a) Spinal nerve root 1 exits from above the C1 vertebra.
(b) C6-C7 disc impingement typically impings on root 7.
(c) Outside the intervertebral foramen, spinal nerves re-branch into rami.
(d) Dorsal rami are somatic, whereas rami communicantes are visceral.
(e) Ventral rami serve the muscles and skin of the back in a neatly segmented pattern.

Set 3 Concerning Nerve Plexuses and Innervation

(a) Dorsal rami form the major limb plexuses.
(b) Thoracic ventral rami do not form nerve plexuses.
(c) The four main nerve plexuses are Cervical, Brachial, Lumbar, and Sacral.
(d) Networks of ventral rami exchange fibers to innervate mainly the limbs.
(e) In the thorax, ventral rami continue as intercostal nerves.

Set 4 Regarding Cranial Nerves and Foramina

(a) All 12 pairs of cranial nerves arise directly from the brainstem.
(b) The optic, olfactory, and vestibulocochlear nerves are purely sensory.
(c) CN III, IV, V1, and VI all pass through the superior orbital fissure.
(d) The facial nerve (CN VII) passes through the internal auditory canal.
(e) CN XII (Hypoglossal) passes through the jugular foramen.

Set 5 Concerning Clinical Testing of Cranial Nerves

(a) Testing CN I usually involves sniffing odorous substances like coffee grounds or vanilla.

(d) CN VII testing involves checking facial symmetry, wrinkling the forehead, and taste on the anterior 2/3 of the tongue.
(e) CN XI is tested by having the patient stick their tongue out straight.
